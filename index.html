Mexico City 🇲🇽

My picks for eating, exploring, and getting around CDMX

Where to eat & drink

Start at the taco stands, end with mezcal.

Tacos

El Califa de León

Tiny Michelin-recognized taqueria in San Rafael. Get the gaonera.

Tacos

Taquería Orinoco

Northern-style trompo and chicharrón tacos, open late.

Fine dining

Pujol

Enrique Olvera's tasting menu, famous for the 2,000+ day mole madre. Book weeks ahead.

Mexican

Contramar

Long, lively lunch. Order the tuna tostadas and pescado a la talla.

Market

Mercado Roma & Mercado de Coyoacán

Grazing food halls. Coyoacán is more local, with great tostadas.

Breakfast

Churrería El Moro

Churros and thick hot chocolate since 1935, open 24 hours.

Coffee

Quentin Café & Cafebrería El Péndulo

Great Mexican beans in Roma/Condesa. Péndulo is a bookstore café.

Bar

Bar Opera & La Clandestina

Historic cantina vibes downtown, and a serious mezcal list in Condesa.

What to see

Mix history, art, and neighborhood wandering.

History

Zócalo & Templo Mayor

The historic center and the Aztec ruins buried beneath it.

Museum

Museo Nacional de Antropología

One of the world's best museums. Allow 3+ hours.

Art

Museo Frida Kahlo (Casa Azul)

Buy timed tickets online, well in advance.

Park

Bosque de Chapultepec

Huge park with the castle, lake, and several museums.

Architecture

Palacio de Bellas Artes

Art Nouveau/Deco opera house with Rivera and Orozco murals.

Stroll

Roma Norte & Condesa

Tree-lined streets, Art Deco buildings, bookshops, and cafés.

Boats

Xochimilco

Colorful trajinera boats on the canals. Go on a weekday to avoid crowds.

Views

Torre Latinoamericana

Observation deck for a skyline view of the whole valley.

Where to stay

Pick a neighborhood first, then a hotel.

Best all-round

Roma Norte / Condesa

Walkable, safe, packed with food and nightlife. My top pick for first-timers.

Upscale

Polanco

Luxury hotels, big-name restaurants, near Chapultepec and museums.

Historic

Centro Histórico

Close to major sights; lively by day, quieter and less polished at night.

Day trips

Ruins

Teotihuacán

Climb the Pyramid of the Sun. Go early to beat heat and crowds (~1 hr away).

Town

Puebla

Colonial city known for mole poblano and talavera pottery.

Town

Cuernavaca / Taxco

Weekend-style escapes: gardens in Cuernavaca, silver shops in Taxco.

Practical tips

  • Altitude: CDMX is ~7,350 ft. Hydrate and take it easy on day one.
  • Getting around: Use Uber/DiDi or the Metro (cheap, crowded at rush hour). Avoid hailing street taxis.
  • Water: Stick to bottled or purified water; ice at restaurants is generally fine.
  • Money: Cards work in most places; carry pesos for markets and taco stands.
  • Best time: March–May is warm and dry; November–February is cooler and clear. Rain falls most afternoons June–September.
  • Safety: Normal big-city precautions: watch belongings on the Metro, keep your phone out of sight on the street.
  • Museums: Many are closed on Mondays.
Hours, prices, and availability change, so double-check before you go. Have a great trip! 🌮
